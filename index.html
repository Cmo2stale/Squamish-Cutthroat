CCT Spawner Survey

A weekly walk of each creek in the spawning window. Count new redds, note whether adults are on them, and record every visit, even when nothing is found. Works with no signal.

Surveys

Season by creek

New redds are counted once, on the visit they were first flagged. "With adults" is redds where adults were present when found or re-checked. Peak adults is the highest count of adult cutthroat on a single visit.

How to survey

method
  1. Same reach, about every 7 days, February to May. Cutthroat redds stay visible for about two weeks (average 13 days, none longer than 20), so a longer gap misses redds.
  2. Go as flows drop. Spawning appears to pause during freshets and peak as the water falls. If you can't see the bottom of riffles, record the visit as visibility 3 and come back.
  3. Walk upstream, slowly, off the gravel. Wear polarized glasses and a cap. Never step on a redd.
  4. Flag every new redd. Write the redd ID and date on flagging tied on the bank just upstream. On the next visit, check each old flag: still visible or gone. Only unflagged redds are new.
  5. For every redd: confidence, and adults present or absent. Adults are rarely on redds. In a six-year cutthroat study, fewer than 3% of 544 redds had a fish on them. "Absent" is the usual answer and it's still useful data.
  6. Count adult cutthroat in the reach. Count only fish about 25 cm or longer. Smaller fish are likely parr or smolts.
  7. If you see a spawning pair, record it carefully. Note the time, count, behaviour and a photo or video. It may be one of the first documented in the watershed.
  8. Record nil visits. A walk with no redds and no adults is a record. Repeated visits are how the project measures absence and how often adults are missed.
  9. Keep the same crew on the same creeks. Redd counts vary with experience, so consistent surveyors make years comparable.

Based on the weekly redd-count method of Losee, Phillips & Young (2016, Skookum Creek, Puget Sound), which followed the Gallagher & Gallagher (2005) protocol. The redd re-check and confidence follow Gallagher, Hahn & Johnson (2007, AFS Salmonid Field Protocols Handbook) and ODFW's 2026 spawning-survey manual. The visibility codes are ODFW's. The volunteer-friendly form layout follows Trout Unlimited's Redd Survey Handbook (2019).

File type

Four tables: a season summary by creek, every survey visit, every redd with its re-check history, and a detections table (one row per visit: redds found 1/0, adults seen 1/0) laid out for occupancy and detection analysis.

Backup

Surveys live only on this phone. Save a backup after each field day.

How backups work
  • What it is: one file with every survey and redd on this phone.
  • Where to keep it: email it to yourself or the project lead, or save it to Files, Google Drive or iCloud.
  • It looks strange, and that's normal. It's the app's own format, not a spreadsheet. Use Export for a readable copy.
  • Don't edit it. Changing anything can stop it from restoring.
  • To restore: tap Restore from a backup and pick the file. Restoring only adds or updates. It never deletes.
Your data

Nothing is uploaded. There are no accounts or servers. Surveys stay on this phone until a crew member exports or backs them up and chooses who to send them to.
