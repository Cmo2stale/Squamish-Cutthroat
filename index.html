Measuring a redd

Length runs from the upstream edge of the pit to the downstream end of the tailspill mound. Width is the widest point across the disturbed gravel. Both in centimetres.

Cutthroat pits run roughly 20–75 cm, averaging about 48 × 43 cm. Coho redds are considerably larger. Coho will be finished by the cutthroat window, but their redds are still on the bed, so the measurement is how you tell them apart.

Visibility

1 — bottom of riffles and pools visible. 2 — riffles only. 3 — can't see the bottom; not surveyable, so zero redds doesn't mean absence.

The rules that matter
  • Walk upstream so you aren't looking through your own silt.
  • Never step on a redd, or on gravel that might be one.
  • Write the date and the number of redds on the flagging tape.
  • A walk that finds nothing is still a survey. Save it.

Backup

Surveys live only on this phone. Save a backup after each field day.

How backups work
  • What it is: one file with every survey and redd on this phone.
  • Where to keep it: email it to yourself or the project lead, or save it to Files, Drive or iCloud.
  • It looks strange, and that's normal. It's the app's own format, not a spreadsheet. Use Export for a readable copy.
  • Don't edit it. Changing anything can stop it from restoring.
  • To restore: tap Restore from a backup and pick the file. Restoring only adds or updates. It never deletes.
Your data

Nothing is uploaded. There are no accounts and no server. Surveys stay on this phone until a crew member exports or backs them up and chooses who to send them to.
